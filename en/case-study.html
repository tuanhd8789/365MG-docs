Case study

350 users, 3.4 million emails and 670 GB of files to a new tenant

A services company with offices in two countries moved to a new Microsoft 365 tenant after a change of ownership. The first attempt, with scripts run by hand, stalled for weeks. This is what went wrong, how the migration was finished, and what 365MG does about each problem today.

The starting point

Scripts copied one message at a time through Microsoft Graph from a single PC. After a month the target held about 71 % of the mail. Reports said most mailboxes had "caught up", yet some target mailboxes were empty.

  • A controller crashed on a data type error and was restarted every hour by a watchdog, doing nothing in between.
  • 170 target accounts had been deleted and recreated, and 190 journals reset; the sync kept treating those mailboxes as finished.
  • Workers held whole messages with attachments in memory: some reached 3 GB of RAM.
  • One rejected calendar item stopped a whole mailbox on every run.

What fixed it

  • Journals per item checked against live counts in both tenants, so "caught up" meant what it said.
  • Size-aware batches spooled to disk instead of memory: each worker stayed around 200 MB.
  • Rejected items recorded and skipped instead of stopping the mailbox.
  • Adaptive pacing per mailbox when Exchange throttled one target: one mailbox went from 42 to about 200 messages per minute.
  • 24 parallel workers for mail and up to 44 for OneDrive, spread over several data mover apps to stay under per-app limits.

Mail reached 342 of 352 mailboxes matched (99.1 % of messages). The rest were not tool errors: 4 mailboxes whose license the admin had removed on purpose, and 6 mailboxes where messages filed in two folders had been matched to one copy, then repaired.

Lessons built into 365MG

  • Junk Email older than 30 days is deleted by Exchange Online in the target: 365MG skips it by default so counts agree.
  • Large files are downloaded in ranges with their own timeout: a stalled 29.8 GB file no longer hangs a worker.
  • A worker that stops moving is restarted automatically and continues from the journal.
  • Dry runs check target mailboxes and OneDrives before the real copy.
